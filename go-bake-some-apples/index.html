Sociological & Economic Anatomy

The Six Thematic Pillars

The architecture of modern economic deception deconstructed into six core pillars, demonstrating how theological dogma replaces empirical accountability.

Pillar A

The 'Infinite Pie' Myth

The central ideological pillar of modern capitalism is that the market is not a zero-sum contest, but an ever-expanding pie where everyone benefits from aggregate growth. However, by abstracting economic distribution into pure production, it renders systemic concentration of wealth invisible.

Manifestation: 'Growing the pie' political rhetoric, framing wealth redistribution as economically ignorant.
Outcome: Misdirects the working class into endless individual toil ('baking apples') while capital extracts the bakery.
Pillar B

The 'Rising Tide' Fallacy

When a tide rises, it only lifts boats that are intact and seaworthy. For families burdened with predatory student debt, medical crises, and stagnant wages, an economic flood doesn't lift their vessel—it swamps and capsizes it.

Manifestation: 'Trickle-down economics' and supply-side deregulation asserting all strata rise equally with top-end investment.
Outcome: A reality of rising yachts and sinking rowboats. Since 1989, the top 1% gained $21 Trillion in net worth, while the bottom 50% lost $900 Billion.
Pillar C

The Doctrine: Supply-Side & The Laffer Curve

The Laffer Curve functions not as empirical economics, but as theological dogma. Like a tent revival sermon, it promises salvation and abundance for the faithful while shielding extreme concentrations of capital from public obligation.

Manifestation: The claim that reducing taxes on capital and top earners will generate higher total government revenues through unbridled growth.
Outcome: Decades of empirical data prove supply-side tax cuts consistently fail to spur productivity or wage gains, while blowing trillions into the national debt.
Pillar D

The Finite Game: Bailouts, Buybacks & Tax Cuts

While the working class is told the economy is an infinite game of mutual creation, corporate executives and private equity play a ruthless, finite zero-sum game of extraction, using financial leverage, regulatory capture, and state bailouts to guarantee their profits.

Manifestation: TARP 2008 ($250B to banks vs $31B to homeowners), the 2017 TCJA ($61,090 avg cut for the 1% vs $70 for the bottom 20%), and corporate stock buybacks consuming 94% of corporate profits.
Outcome: Socialism for the wealthy, cutthroat capitalism for the working class. Forty years of wage stagnation paired with massive upward wealth extraction.
Pillar E

Manufacturing Consent: False Consciousness & Culture Wars

As analyzed by Thomas Frank in 'What's the Matter with Kansas?', when economic inequality reaches destabilizing extremes, the system performs a systematic erasure of the economic, substituting identity battles so workers fight each other instead of the bakery owners.

Manifestation: Distraction via cultural grievances, billionaire-owned cable news echo chambers, and weaponizing social divides to redefine 'elites' as professors and artists rather than oligarchs.
Outcome: Working-class families vote against their own healthcare, pensions, and safety nets, turning their valid fury upon their own neighbors.
Pillar F

The 'Human Waste' of the Finite Game

In a finite game, players play to win, and for there to be winners, there must be losers. The discarded worker is not an accident of the machine; they are its calculated byproduct. Awakening begins when the discarded realize their shared humanity and refuse to play the finite game.

Manifestation: The laid-off manufacturing worker, the displaced farmer, the medically bankrupt parent, and the millions denied dental and preventative care.
Outcome: Human lives treated as expendable fuel for short-term balance sheet optimization. The 'infinite pie' is the palliative lie told to the discarded.

Album Liner Notes

Listen Closely. Question the Bakery.

Welcome to "Go Bake Some Apples."

I look at the world the way I’ve always looked at complex architectures: I track the inputs, the processing, and the outputs. When an entire class of people is struggling to stay above water, I don't believe it's an accident. When a system fails its people, it is usually by design.

This album is a dark folk satire that deconstructs the central, public-facing myth of modern American capitalism: the "infinite pie". First formulated to justify free-market inequality, this myth asserts that the economy always grows, creating more for everyone, and framing any call for wealth redistribution as economically ignorant. The most poetic version of this lie is the idea that a "rising tide lifts all boats".

But when you look at the raw data, the mechanics of this system tell a devastatingly different story. Instead of lifting all boats, the last forty years have been a story of rising yachts and sinking rowboats. Since 1989, the top 1% has gained $21 Trillion in net worth, while the bottom 50% has lost $900 Billion.

"Go Bake Some Apples" follows the psychological and economic journey of a single protagonist. He begins as a true believer in the trickle-down gospel, convinced that if he just keeps his head down and works hard, his slice of the pie will manifest. He scoffs at hand-outs and trusts the system. But as reality collides with the myth—through medical debt, wage stagnation, and ultimately job loss—his life is systematically dismantled by the very people he was taught to revere.

He discovers that while the working class is kept busy "baking apples," the elite are playing a ruthless, zero-sum "finite game" of extraction and power. He sees the massive 2008 bank bailouts that handed ~$250 billion to banks while disbursing a fraction to struggling homeowners. He sees the 2017 tax cuts that handed the top 1% an average cut of $61,090 while tossing the bottom 20% a mere $70. He watches corporations spend 94% of their profits on stock buybacks to inflate CEO bonuses rather than paying living wages.

To keep him from fighting back, the system performs a "systematic erasure of the economic," feeding him a manufactured culture war. He is trained by billionaire-owned media to aim his valid anger at his neighbors, teachers, and marginalized groups rather than the architects of his poverty.

Through twelve tracks of Americana, folk-blues, and aggressive industrial-folk, this album traces his path from false consciousness to bitter, furious emancipation. It is a story about turning off the television, unmasking the merchants of outrage, and realizing that we don't need to beg for a slice of their finite pie. We need to play the infinite game. We need to build a new table entirely.

Listen closely. Question the bakery.

- The Bard

Spotlight: 'My Dentist Is a Communist'

When Ideology Collides with a Rotting Nerve

Sometimes the deepest brainwashing collides with a raw nerve. In 'My Dentist Is a Communist,' our protagonist has hit absolute rock bottom. He is in blinding, physical agony from an infected tooth, but he simply cannot afford dental care. Yet, because he has been fed a steady diet of manufactured culture-war grievances, he doesn't blame the economic machine that priced him out of human healthcare. Instead, he sits in his darkened kitchen, gulping ice water, and using his remaining breath to repeat the exact political talking points that put him in the dark. He brands his dentist a 'communist' for charging a fee and denounces public safety nets as 'tyranny.' It is the tragic, absurd collision between a failing economic doctrine and a man's own nervous system—the ultimate portrait of false consciousness defending the very machinery that extracts its life.
