Bob McNair

Living Working-Class & Highland History: From 1746 Loch Fyne to the 1907 Greenock Slate

Through his family heirlooms and living oral history, friend and subscriber Bob McNair opened a window into two centuries of Scottish endurance—from his ancestors serving as the MacNair tacksmen defending the Cowal shoreline after Culloden in 1746, to his Great Uncle Wullie Smith McNair and Granddad Nicol John slating the Greenock Townhall roof in 1907 before the Great War. His accounts inspired the first two flagship releases in The Singles Vault:

Single #01 • 1907–Present Chronicle 06:59

The Greenock Slate and the Gold Watch

Great Uncle Wullie Smith McNair, Granddad Nicol John, the forgotten “pieces” on Greenock Townhall roof, WWI Pals battalions, climbing the high slate on one good leg, and the retinal gold pocket watch ticking in Bob’s hand today.

The Guns of Loch Fyne

After Chief Lachlan MacLachlan fell at Culloden (Drumossie Moor), Captain Fergussone sailed HMS Furnace up Loch Fyne to shell Old Castle Lachlan—while the MacNair tacksmen held the shallows with musket and lead to shield the glen.
