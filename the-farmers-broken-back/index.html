Historical Milestone · 1973–1985

Exhibit I: The Expansion Trap & The 1980s Interest Spike

In 1973, USDA Secretary Earl Butz urged American farmers to plant “fencerow to fencerow” to meet export demand from the Soviet Union. Guaranteed land appreciation and bank cheerleading drove unprecedented debt accumulation. But in 1979, the Federal Reserve under Paul Volcker hiked prime interest rates to 21.5% to curb inflation, while President Carter imposed a Soviet grain embargo in 1980.

Land values plummeted by more than 60% across the Corn Belt overnight. Farmers who held 80% equity on paper saw their collateral vanish while variable-rate debt skyrocketed. The resulting tidal wave of foreclosures precipitated the deepest rural crisis since the Great Depression and altered the demographic fabric of the heartland forever.

Economic Reality · USDA ERS Data

Exhibit II: The 14.3¢ Food Dollar & The Big Four

According to the USDA Economic Research Service, the farmer’s share of the retail consumer food dollar has steadily eroded from over 50 cents in the 1950s to just 14.3 cents today. The remaining 85.7 cents is captured by processors, packaging conglomerates, transportation logistics, distributors, and mega-retailers.

In beef packing, just four multinational corporations (Tyson, JBS, Cargill, and National Beef) control over 85% of steer and heifer slaughter. This extreme market concentration creates a captive buyer bottleneck: ranchers and feedlots have no alternative buyers, forcing them to accept predatory grid-pricing formulas while supermarket meat prices soar.

Technological Enclosure · Seed & Software

Exhibit III: The Digital Leash & Sterile Seed Patents

The shift from traditional capital equipment to corporate-controlled technological enclosures has redefined property rights on the farm. Modern tractors, combines, and sprayers are equipped with digital rights management (DRM) and Controller Area Network (CAN) bus encryption that lock farmers out of independent diagnostic repairs. When an arbitrary “Code 502” sensor error triggers during harvest, the machine halts until a certified dealer travels out with proprietary software keys.

Simultaneously, seed patents on genetically modified traits prohibit the ancient practice of seed saving. Farmers purchase one-time usage licenses alongside mandatory chemical packages, subject to private corporate investigators and patent infringement litigation.

Agrarian Policy · The Path Forward

Exhibit IV: Bust the Trust & Parity Restoration

True agrarian renewal does not lie in emergency corporate bailout checks, but in systemic antitrust enforcement, supply-demand management, and mandatory market transparency. Drawing on the legacy of the 1890 Sherman Antitrust Act, the 1921 Packers and Stockyards Act, and the original 1933 Agricultural Adjustment Act, family farm advocates demand the revival of agricultural parity.

Key policy remediation includes breaking up meatpacking and seed monopolies, passing federal Right to Repair legislation for all farm equipment, banning predatory tournament systems in contract poultry production, and establishing national strategic grain reserves that decouple farm livelihood from Wall Street commodity speculation.
